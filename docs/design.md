# Workbench design

Audience: a pricing analyst or an interview reviewer learning how a price decision is made. One job: explain and calculate a bounded price proposal from visible inputs.

Palette: slate ink #23323d, steel background #edf1f4, paper #ffffff, workshop orange #bc4b19, teal #17686c, pale warning #fff2e7. Typography: condensed system sans for major labels, Segoe UI/Arial for body, tabular monospace for prices. No remote fonts or runtime CDN.

The signature is a price rail: cost/margin floor and change/market ceilings visibly narrow the available price range. The calculation stays beside its input, like a technician's workbench. Three columns collapse to a vertical task sequence on mobile.

Initial generic dashboard idea was rejected: counts and decorative KPI cards would distract from understanding one price. Keep one real product, one set of explicit assumptions and one decision visible. Labels distinguish historical facts, user assumptions and calculated scenarios.
